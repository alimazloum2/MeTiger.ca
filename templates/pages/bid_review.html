Who it's for

Who an electrical bid review is for

A bid review is not a sign you got it wrong — it's how experienced teams protect margin on the bids that matter most.

Contractors racing a deadline

The tender closes in hours and the count was rushed. We catch what the pressure hid before you commit to a number you'll live with.

GCs levelling sub bids

Three quotes, three different scopes. We help you see which bid is actually missing scope rather than simply cheaper.

Estimators wanting a check

A clean estimate deserves a senior, independent second opinion on your quantities and assumptions before it reaches the client.

Deliverables

What you get back

A marked-up review

Your drawings and takeoff annotated where we see risk — colour-coded so you can jump straight to what needs a decision.

A scope-gap & RFI log

Every gap, conflict, and open question in one list — ready to send to the design team or fold into your exclusions.

A written risk summary

A short, plain-language rundown of the risks we found and what we'd recommend — the whole picture on one page.

A bid review checks the estimate you already built — it is not a re-estimate. We do not rebuild your number from scratch unless you ask us to. If you'd rather have an independent takeoff or a full estimate, that's a separate service we're glad to quote.
The problem

Why bids get missed

The most expensive misses are rarely a wrong price — they're scope that was never counted at all, hidden in the seams between documents.

  • Equipment power buried in the mechanical, kitchen, and process drawings
  • Late addenda that shifted scope after the takeoff was done
  • A tight deadline that forced a fast count with no time to check it
Approach

CEC-aware, honest about scope

Our review is informed by CSA C22.1 / the Canadian Electrical Code, provincial practices, project specifications, and local utility requirements. We flag where the documents disagree — we don't rule on code.

Final compliance remains subject to the project engineer, the local authority having jurisdiction (AHJ), and applicable provincial/territorial requirements. MeTiger provides estimating and coordination support — not stamped drawings, sealed engineering, or code-compliance certification — and works alongside the engineer of record where required.
