How remote estimating works across Newfoundland & Labrador

We work with electrical contractors, general contractors, construction managers, and design-build teams pricing projects in St. John’s, Mount Pearl, Corner Brook, Gander, and Grand Falls-Windsor — and on the remote coastal and Labrador sites where a careful count against a bid deadline is hardest to get.

Because every estimate is built from your PDF or DWG tender set, geography never changes the work. A project off the road network on the Northern Peninsula or a facility in Labrador is handled with the same discipline, deliverables, and fee basis as one in the St. John’s metro area. You send the documents and the due date; we return a reviewable package on an agreed window.

Provincial context

Electrical estimating built for Newfoundland & Labrador projects

Electrical work in Newfoundland & Labrador is governed by the Canadian Electrical Code (CSA C22.1) as adopted in the province, with electrical inspection and permitting administered provincially through the local authority having jurisdiction. We estimate what the tender documents call for and reconcile them against those requirements.

On the utility side, most island projects connect through Newfoundland Power on the interconnected system, with Newfoundland & Labrador Hydro supplying generation and serving many rural, coastal, and Labrador loads. Service coordination, metering, and utility-interface notes are part of how we frame the electrical scope, so your service and distribution numbers hold together before you commit to a price.

Estimating and coordination support only. Final compliance remains subject to the project engineer, the Newfoundland & Labrador authority having jurisdiction, and applicable provincial requirements — MeTiger works alongside the engineer of record where required and does not provide stamped or sealed engineering.

Code & compliance

Newfoundland & Labrador code & compliance

Our estimating and scope review are informed by CSA C22.1 / the Canadian Electrical Code as adopted in Newfoundland & Labrador, together with project specifications, local utility requirements, and the bid documents. We estimate what the documents call for and flag where they don’t agree — so pricing rests on a defensible reading of scope, not guesswork.

MeTiger provides CEC-aware estimating and coordination support — not stamped drawings, sealed or professional engineering, permit-ready engineering, or code-compliance certification. Final compliance remains subject to the project engineer, the Newfoundland & Labrador authority having jurisdiction, and applicable provincial requirements. We work alongside the engineer of record where required.
