Privacy

What is anonymized

A sample should show our format and discipline, never a real client's data. Before we share an example, we strip anything that could identify the project or expose pricing that isn't ours to give out.

  • Project identity and job name removed
  • Client and contractor names removed
  • Site addresses and drawing title blocks blurred
  • Proprietary and client-specific pricing removed or blurred
  • Confidential specification details generalized
The sample is illustrative of format and structure only. It is not a priced quote for your project, and it does not represent any specific client engagement.
Components

Sample estimate components

Each component in the package does a job. Together they let your team check the number instead of trusting it.

Takeoff workbook

A structured quantity takeoff with device, feeder, gear, and fixture counts grouped by system, so every line traces back to a drawing.

Marked-up drawings

Colour-coded plans where each counted item is highlighted, letting you audit coverage sheet by sheet against the takeoff.

Material list by system

Materials organized by service, distribution, power, lighting, and low-voltage, using Canadian market pricing assumptions.

Labour-hour summary

Labour-unit based estimating with project-specific labour assumptions and regional labour adjustment, shown so the basis is visible.

Assumptions & exclusions

A plain list of what is included, what is carried as an assumption, and what is explicitly out of scope — no silent guesses.

RFI & scope-gap log

Items that appear in one document but not another, equipment with no clear power source, and conflicts you can send to the design team.

Bid summary

A single reviewable summary rolling material and labour together, with alternates and value-engineering notes where they apply.

CEC-aware scope review

Scope review informed by CSA C22.1 / the Canadian Electrical Code, provincial practices, and the bid documents.
