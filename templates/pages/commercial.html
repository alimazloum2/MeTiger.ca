Coordination

The coordination realities that move a commercial number

Commercial electrical margin usually leaks at the boundaries — between the base building and the tenant, and between the electrical drawings and every other trade. We estimate those boundaries on purpose instead of assuming them away.

  • Base-building versus tenant-improvement scope kept on separate lines so you price your contract, not the landlord's
  • Mechanical, plumbing, kitchen, and specialty equipment power reconciled against the electrical schedules
  • Phased and occupied-space sequencing, temporary power, and after-hours assumptions written into the labour basis
  • Landlord-provided, utility, and existing-to-remain items called out as exclusions, not silent guesses
  • Lighting, controls, and life-safety devices cross-checked against reflected ceiling and fire-alarm sheets
Approach

A CEC-aware approach to commercial scope

Our commercial estimating and Canadian Electrical Code-informed scope review are guided by CSA C22.1 / the Canadian Electrical Code, provincial amendments and practices, project specifications, local utility requirements, and the bid documents. We quantify what the documents call for and flag where they conflict — using regional labour adjustment and project-specific labour assumptions built for Canadian market pricing.

Final compliance remains subject to the project engineer, the local authority having jurisdiction (AHJ), and applicable provincial/territorial requirements. MeTiger provides estimating and coordination support — not stamped drawings, sealed engineering, permit-ready engineering, or code-compliance certification — and works alongside the engineer of record where required.
