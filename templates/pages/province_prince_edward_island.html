Local context

Electrical estimating built for Prince Edward Island projects

Prince Edward Island electrical work follows the Canadian Electrical Code (CSA C22.1) as adopted in the province, with inspection and permitting handled through the provincial electrical inspection authority and the local authority having jurisdiction. We estimate to what the tender documents call for and keep that provincial context in view while we count.

Across the Island, most projects connect to the Maritime Electric distribution network, and service entrance, metering, and utility-coordination details can shape the electrical scope on any given job. We account for the servicing shown on your drawings, note where the utility interface is left unclear, and flag it so the question can be settled before bid day rather than after award. For a smaller market, that discipline matters: remote delivery is what makes experienced estimating support practical for Island contractors who do not carry a full-time estimator for every tender.

Code & compliance

Prince Edward Island code & compliance

Our estimating and scope review are informed by the Canadian Electrical Code (CSA C22.1) as adopted in Prince Edward Island, alongside the project specifications, drawings, addenda, and Maritime Electric utility requirements. We estimate what the documents describe and note where they conflict or leave a gap, so nothing is priced on a silent assumption.

MeTiger provides CEC-aware estimating and coordination support only — not stamped drawings, sealed or professional engineering, permit-ready engineering, or code-compliance certification. Final compliance remains subject to the project engineer, the Prince Edward Island authority having jurisdiction, and applicable provincial requirements. We work alongside the engineer of record where required.
