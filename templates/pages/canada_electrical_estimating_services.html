The problem

Why electrical bids get missed

Electrical scope hides in the gaps between documents. The most common places margin leaks out before the bid even goes in:

  • Missed items between drawings, specifications, and schedules
  • Equipment power requirements buried in the mechanical and other trades’ documents
  • Addenda that change scope late and never make it into the count
  • Incomplete or conflicting drawings with no documented assumptions
  • Weak takeoffs that can’t be checked line by line
  • Tight deadlines that force a rushed, incomplete count

Approach

CEC-aware estimating approach

Canadian electrical estimating support informed by CSA C22.1 / the Canadian Electrical Code requirements, provincial practices, project specifications, local utility requirements, and bid documents. We estimate what the documents call for and flag where they don’t agree.

Final compliance remains subject to the project engineer, the local authority having jurisdiction, and applicable provincial/territorial requirements. MeTiger provides estimating and design coordination support — not a replacement for stamped engineering or authority-having-jurisdiction review — and works alongside the engineer of record where required.
Scope-gap review

We flag what’s missing before you price it

Every package includes a scope-gap and RFI log: items that appear in one document but not another, equipment with no clear power source, details that conflict, and assumptions we had to make to complete the count. You see the gaps instead of inheriting them after award.

  • Cross-checks between drawings, specifications, schedules, and addenda
  • Other-trade equipment connections reconciled against the electrical scope
  • Documented assumptions and exclusions, not silent guesses
  • An RFI list you can send straight to the design team
