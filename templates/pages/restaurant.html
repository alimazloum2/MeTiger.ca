Coordination

Tenant scope vs landlord base building

On most restaurant fit-outs the biggest pricing risk isn't the count — it's the line between what the landlord provides and what the tenant carries. We read the lease work letter and base-building set alongside the tenant drawings so nothing is double-counted or quietly missed.

  • Service size, panel location, and metering provided under the base building
  • Feeders, distribution, and fit-out power carried by the tenant
  • Demising-wall, roof, and shared-corridor responsibilities reconciled
  • Landlord allowances and back-charges flagged against the tenant scope

Different tenant and base-building responsibilities show up as documented assumptions in the estimate, so your team sees the split instead of inheriting it after award.

Approach

CEC-aware restaurant estimating

Estimating support informed by CSA C22.1 / the Canadian Electrical Code, provincial amendments, the health-authority and food-service requirements referenced in the documents, project specifications, and local utility rules. We estimate what the documents call for and flag where the equipment schedule and the electrical drawings don't agree.

Final compliance remains subject to the project engineer, the local authority having jurisdiction (AHJ), and applicable provincial/territorial requirements. MeTiger provides electrical estimating and coordination support — not stamped drawings, sealed engineering, or code-compliance certification — and works alongside the engineer of record where required.
